# Order-sensitive bounds for the compact checker

The compact interval checker can use the number of remaining vertices to
strengthen its edge lower bounds. The table is generated by
[order_edge_bounds.py](scripts/order_edge_bounds.py), using only the earlier
global edge lower bounds, the preliminary bound $f(11)\ge52$, and the two
arguments below. It uses no 52-edge profile exclusion and no catalogue of
34-edge or 35-edge graphs.

## Vertex deletion and averaging

Let $G$ have $n$ vertices, $m$ edges, and treewidth at least $k$. If every
$(n-1)$-vertex graph of treewidth at least $k-1$ needs at least $b$ edges,
then deleting any vertex gives

$$\deg_G(v)\le m-b.$$

Summing gives $2m\le n(m-b)$. For $n>2$ this implies

$$m\ge\left\lceil\frac{nb}{n-2}\right\rceil.$$

The table is built in increasing $k$, so each entry depends only on already
proved entries for smaller treewidth. A state with edge upper bound $E$
also gets the degree cap $E-b$ directly. No graph of order at most $k$ has
treewidth at least $k$.

## The small-order lemma

For $1\le k\le11$, any graph of treewidth at least $k$ on at most $k+3$
vertices has at least $\binom{k+1}{2}$ edges.

Add isolated vertices to reach $n=k+3$, and let $F$ be the complement.
First observe that if the complement of an $r$-vertex graph has an edge
$ab$ with both endpoints of degree at least two, that graph has treewidth
at most $r-3$. Eliminate $a$ in the original graph: its degree is at most
$r-3$. A complement edge $bc$ with $c\ne a$ survives, because $b$ was not
a neighbor of $a$ in the original graph. The remaining graph is not
complete, so its treewidth is at most $r-3$ as well.

The 3-core of $F$ cannot contain a triangle or a four-cycle. Suppose it
does and choose a vertex $v$ of the cycle. Its original-graph degree is
at most $n-4$. Eliminating $v$ preserves every complement edge incident
to a complement neighbor of $v$, except the edges to $v$ itself. In a
triangle $vab$, the edge $ab$ survives with both endpoints of complement
degree at least two. In a four-cycle $vabc$, the edge $ab$ survives: $a$
retains at least two complement neighbors, and $b$ retains $a,c$.
The observation above then gives treewidth at most $n-4$, even after
prepending $v$. This contradicts the assumed lower bound $n-3$.

A graph with girth at least five, order $r$, and $q$ edges satisfies

$$\sum_x\deg(x)^2\le r(r-1),\qquad 4q^2\le r^2(r-1).$$

Indeed, counting distinct vertices at distance at most two from each vertex
and then summing gives the first inequality; Cauchy--Schwarz gives the
second. For $2\le r\le14$, having $q\ge2r-2$ would imply
$16(r-1)\le r^2$, whereas $16(r-1)-r^2=48-(r-8)^2>0$ throughout that range.
Thus a nonempty 3-core of $F$ has at most $2r-3$ edges. Adding back each
peeled vertex adds at most two edges. If the core is empty, $F$ is
2-degenerate and has at most $2n-3$ edges directly. In either case,

$$|E(G)|\ge\binom n2-(2n-3)=\binom{n-2}{2}=\binom{k+1}{2}.$$

The restriction $k\le11$ ensures $n\le14$ in this argument.

## Use in the checker

The checker takes the maximum of the global bound, the small-order bound
when applicable, and the averaging bound. It uses the resulting table for
edge contradictions and degree caps. Propagation based on equality with
a **global** edge minimum still compares against the global table, not
against the stronger order-sensitive table: that distinction is essential
when elimination changes the order.

For example, treewidth ten requires at least 49 edges at order fourteen
and at least 48 edges at order sixteen. The table still gives only 52 for
treewidth eleven at orders 21 through 26. Those cases must be excluded
separately; their exclusion has not been smuggled into a premise.
